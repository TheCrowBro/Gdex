# Tourbook

**Tourbook is an app for music producers and the bands they play in, manage or produce.**

It keeps your shows, studio sessions and rehearsals in one calendar, with your acts, the people you work with, your
backline, the venues and studios you play, and your songs and setlists. On the day it gives the band a day sheet, tunes
your guitar, counts you in and times your set. Afterwards it settles the night, splits the money and makes the invoice.

It works on a phone and on a computer, in the browser or installed like an app, and keeps working with no signal. There
is no account: your information stays on your own phone or computer unless you choose to save a backup or send a link.
If you connect your own Google Drive (free, for everyone), Tourbook keeps your phone and computer the same through a private
Tourbook folder there, with a dated copy each day. The **Audiophile** role is free for good, for your own personal use
(not for work); the working roles (producer, manager, band, solo artist, scout, and Jack of Trades), for work too, are
free for 30 days on each device, then need a licence: 20 US dollars once, for up to 3 of your devices. The licence is
built but switched off: until it is switched on, every role is free and nothing is asked. A desktop app for Windows, Mac
and Linux is built too (`docs/desktop.md`); neither it nor the licence is published yet.

Tourbook is a sibling of Vdex, the app for videographers, and shares its look and the way it works.

Tourbook was called Gdex until just before its launch. Its GitHub repository is still TheCrowBro/Gdex, which the owner
may rename on GitHub; nothing here depends on the repository's name.

## Start here

1. **Open Tourbook** at https://tourbook.crowmade.app in your phone's or computer's browser. (The person who looks after
   the tech can also run it: see *For the person who looks after the tech* below.)
2. **Put it on your home screen** so it opens like a normal app, even with no signal:
   - **iPhone:** open it in Safari, tap **Share** (the square with an arrow), then **Add to Home Screen**.
   - **Android and computers:** tap **Install** (⤓) at the top of the page.
3. **Tap ? at the top of any page.** It walks you through that page, box by box.

## Set up for the way you work

The first time Tourbook opens it asks who's using it: **Jack of Trades** (Master of None: a bit of all of it), or
**producer, manager, band, solo artist, scout or audiophile**. Each gets its own menu and pages. Whoever you are, Tourbook opens
on the calendar every time, and the calendar is the first track on every menu:

| Role | Menu (Side A, then Side B) | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| 🧰 Jack of Trades (Master of None) | Bookings · Acts · People · Songs · Studio log · Backline · Venues · Money · Tools · Insurance | Every section on the menu and every box on a booking's page, as Tourbook has always been. Close the question without picking and this is what you get. |
| 🎛️ Producer | Bookings · Studio log · Songs · Artists · Backline · Money · People · Venues · Tools | + New starts a studio session; a session opens with the clock and take log; Tools opens on tap tempo. |
| 💼 Manager | Bookings · Roster · Backline · Money · People · Venues · Insurance | A show opens with the deal and the advance checklist; new people start as industry contacts. |
| 🥁 Band | Bookings · Songs · Backline · Tools · Bands · Money · People · Venues | A show opens with the running order and setlist; Songs opens on your setlists. |
| 🎤 Solo artist | Bookings · Songs · Money · My act · Venues · People · Tools | A show opens with the deal and setlist; private gigs (weddings, parties) are shows too. |
| 🔭 Scout | Shows · Acts · People · Venues | Acts sorted by your latest score; after a show Tourbook asks if you saw them and takes your score; no fees or settlements. |
| 🎧 Audiophile | Gigs · Bands · Venues · Tools | Bands you've seen, most recent first; after a gig Tourbook asks if you saw them and takes your score; Tools opens on the sound meter; no fees. Free, for personal use, not for work. |

**The Audiophile starts with a gig.** Pick Audiophile the first time Tourbook opens and it walks you through adding a
band, then a venue, then a gig at them, each with its own form; a short tour of the app follows. Bringing back a backup
or connecting Google Drive, as on a new phone, takes you straight in instead.

Every menu ends with **My card** and **Settings**, whoever you are. Nothing is ever deleted or out of reach: the
sections a role doesn't use are **bonus tracks** on the sleeve (tap the record), and the booking boxes it doesn't use
are under **More boxes**. Change role any time in **Settings → Your role**.

## What the cards are

The record player at the top is how you move around: every card is a track, Side A and Side B. Swipe the record or tap
⏮ ⏭ to change card, or tap the record to see them all on the sleeve and jump straight to one. Prefer a tape deck, or a
slim digital player with every section one tap away in a row under it? Tap **Aa** at the top, then **Section menu**.

| Card | What it's for |
|---|---|
| 📅 **Bookings** | Shows, studio sessions and rehearsals on one calendar, by month, two weeks, week, three days or day. Open one for everything about it. |
| 🎸 **Acts** | The bands and artists you work with, as drop-down cards grouped by main genre (Rock, Pop, Hip hop & rap…): how many times you have seen each one play, your scores out of 100 (average and latest), and their dates coming up on the calendar. Open a card for the log of each time seen. Each act keeps its members, links, press kit and tech rider with the input list and stage plot. |
| 👥 **People** | Band members, session players, crew, promoters and agents, and how to reach them. |
| 🎼 **Songs** | Keys, tempos and lengths, folded by band and album; split sheets for the writers; setlists that add up to your slot. |
| 🎙️ **Studio log** | A take log for recording: tap + Take, mark the keepers, note what to fix. |
| 🎛️ **Backline** | Instruments, amps, mics and cables with serial numbers and values; stage packs for the gigs you do often. |
| 📍 **Venues** | Venues, studios, rehearsal rooms and festivals: capacity, stage, PA, curfew, noise limit, load-in, map, and the house rules (cameras, age limit, bags…) that show on every show there. |
| 💰 **Money** | What you've earned, what's unpaid, deposits due, settlements and each person's share. |
| 🎚️ **Tools** | A tuner, a metronome, tap tempo with delay times, a sound level meter and a set timer. |
| 🛡️ **Insurance** | Your policies and renewals, and whether your cover matches your backline. |
| 🪪 **My card** | Your contact card as a big QR code, with your name and details under it: someone scans it with their phone camera to save your number and email. **Show big** puts the code alone on a white screen, as big as it fits, and keeps the screen on; **Share** sends the card with your phone's share sheet; **Save as a file** gives you a .vcf; **Edit your details** changes them. |
| ⚙️ **Settings** | Your details (and the way to My card), quotes and invoices, backups, display, and the tour again. |

**A band that changes its name keeps the old one.** Change an act's name and Tourbook asks whether it is a new name or a
spelling fix, and since when. A new name leaves the old one in small type under it, "formerly The Night Owls", on the
act's card and page, and shows and scores from before the change say the name the band had that night; search finds
the band by any of its names. Press kits, riders, day sheets, invoices and calendar files carry the current name only.

**Bands grouped by their main genre.** Once your acts span two or more main genres, the list folds them under one
heading each, with a count: Blues, Classical, Country, Electronic, Folk, Gospel & Christian, Hip hop & rap, Jazz, Latin,
Metal, Pop, Punk, R&B & soul, Reggae & ska, Rock and World, A to Z, then **Unique** for a genre Tourbook doesn't know and
**No genre yet**. Tap a heading to drop its bands down (in the list's own order); the ones you open stay open while
Tourbook is open. You still type the genre as you like ("Indie rock", "Doom folk"): Tourbook works out the main genre from it
(indie rock and folk rock are Rock, doom folk is Folk, the first of two genres decides), and an act's form has **Main
genre: Automatic (Rock)** to set it by hand. The list's search opens the groups with a match, and both searches find a
band by its main genre. The press kit keeps the genre as you typed it.

**A QR code of your sample work, for producers and managers.** An act's form has a **Sample work** box for the address
of your work for them: a SoundCloud, Drive or Dropbox page of your mixes, a private playlist. Drop the act's card open
and it is there as a small QR code with its address (soundcloud.com), **Show big** to put it on the whole screen for
someone to scan, and **Share link** to send it from your phone's share sheet (or copy it). With no sample work link the
code holds the act's first link, and the card says so, with **✎ Edit** to add one; with no link at all it says to add
one in ✎ Edit. Producers, managers and Jack of Trades see it; the other roles don't (a link already typed is kept). It
never goes on the press kit, the rider or anything else you send, and the search doesn't look in it.

**Songs folded by band, then by album.** Each song has an **Album or release** box, which offers the albums already
typed for the same band. With songs of two or more bands (or some with no band), the songs list folds them under each
band, A to Z, with **No act** (in your role's word: No artist, No band) last, and inside a band under each album, A to
Z, with **Singles & unreleased** last for the songs with none (in every band, once any song has an album; until then a
band's fold lists its songs straight under its name). With songs of one band, only its album folds, once a song has an
album. Folded at first; the ones you open stay open while Tourbook is open. The list's search opens the folds with a match,
and both searches find a song by its album. The setlists keep their band chips; the press kit and the printed setlists
are unchanged.

**Each band's gear in a fold, with what you put in.** An item of backline can be **for** one of your acts and **paid
by** you, the band or a person (Paid by left empty is "not known"). For a producer, a manager and Jack of Trades,
Backline then folds the gear by act, A to Z, with **Not for an act** last: each heading says how many items, their total
value (each item's value times how many) and **your investment**, the value of what you paid for yourself. Tap a heading
to drop its gear down; the ones you open stay open while Tourbook is open, and the page's search and the owner chips open
the folds with a match. Producers and managers have Backline on their menu, right after their acts. Every role's item
form has the two boxes, and the item's sheet says what they hold; the figures show only in a role that deals with money.
Money in and out stays on the Money page, and the inventory, stage packs and everything printed are as they were. Search
finds an item by its band's name too.

**Your currency, chosen by country.** At the top of **Settings → Quotes & invoices** (and in Your details and the welcome
tour) one list holds every country with its currency, "United Kingdom — £ GBP", A to Z, with the country your device is
set to first. Picking one sets the symbol on every amount and the code your Pay buttons and bank QR code use, together.
Each quote and invoice has its own **Currency** in its editor, from the same list and starting as yours, for work
abroad: an invoice in euros has € on it and asks for euros with its Pay buttons and bank QR code (Venmo, Cash App and
Monzo, which take only dollars or pounds, open without the amount, and a card page with {amount} is left off), and once
sent a document keeps its currency whatever you change yours to. The Money page never
adds two currencies together: it shows a total for each ("£900 GBP + €400 EUR"). A new device starts in US dollars until
you pick, and a currency typed by hand in an older version stays as it is until you pick a country.

## The calendar

- **Five views:** Month, 2 weeks, Week, 3 days and Day, from the row of buttons above the calendar. Month, 2 weeks
  and Week are day squares (a 2-week square shows each booking's time and the start of its name; on the narrowest
  phones, the time alone); Week is one row of the same squares, taller, so each day shows twice as many bookings;
  3 days and Day put each booking on an hour grid. Tap a day to open it. Tourbook opens
  on the view you picked last, on today. **Coming up** is under every view; in the small hours, last night's show
  that has not played yet stays at its top, marked **Tonight**.
- **In the order the bands play:** bookings on the same day run by the time on stage (doors, soundcheck or load-in
  when there is no set time yet; a session by its start), a set after midnight at the end of the night (a session,
  rehearsal or diary entry before 06:00 is that morning's, first in its day), and anything with no time last. Two
  shows whose sets overlap say so: "Overlaps with Sable, 21:30"; a show over several days says it on the day of the
  clash in the words a Week square gives a screen reader, and with that day on its Coming up card ("Overlaps with
  Sable, Sat 21:30").
- **"Did you see them?"** After a show is over, Tourbook asks when it next opens, or when you come back to it after three
  hours or more away (it stays on the page you left). One list holds every show that is waiting. **Yes** takes your
  score out of 100 for the act's card; **No** marks the show "Didn't go" and never asks again. Change your answer any
  time on the show's own page. It asks producers, managers, scouts and audiophiles (and Jack of Trades); turn it on or
  off for anyone under **Settings → Your role**.
- **House rules:** what a venue allows in (cameras, phones, the age limit, bags, anything unusual) is noted once on the
  venue and shows on every show there as a green, amber or red line, with the full rules on the show's page: what is
  not allowed, what else is restricted (an age limit, cash only), what is allowed and the rest. Green means nothing is
  restricted; anything restricted, or any rule you wrote yourself, is amber; no phones, or no cameras of any kind, is
  red. You can set the colour by hand too, and the show then says so in words. The calendar's **?** explains the line.
  Search Venues by a rule too: "no phones", "cash only", "21+".

## Search

The search box is at the top of every page, just above the section menu (press **/** on a keyboard to jump into it).
Type anything and the results open over the page as you type: bookings (shows, studio sessions, rehearsals and diary
entries, and the times you logged seeing a band that were never on the calendar), bands, venues, people, songs,
setlists, gear, the studio log, and, in a role that deals with money, invoices and quotes. Every word you type has to be
found somewhere, in any order; capitals and accents don't matter.

- **Tabs:** All shows the best matches of every kind in one list, a name that starts with what you typed first. Each
  kind has a tab of its own with how many it found; a kind with nothing is left out.
- **Dates:** type a day ("20 nov", "nov 20", "20/11", "2026-11-20", "friday", "today", "tomorrow") or a span ("nov",
  "november 2025", "2025", "this week", "next week", "last month") and everything dated then comes up: bookings on those
  days, logged times, studio logs, invoices and quotes. A date and words narrow each other: "church 2025" is the shows at
  The Church that year. A band or a place whose name holds a date ("April Wine", "The Saturday Club") still finds all its
  bookings. The results say how the date was read.
- **Bookings** are the calendar's own cards, newest first, with today marked between what is still to come and what is
  over. A band is found by any name it has had, and a card from before it changed its name says "formerly …". A band you
  logged as the support act at a show on the calendar is part of that show's card, with its score. Tap a place to see
  only what was there.
- **Tap a result** to open it where it lives (the band's card, the venue, the person, the song, the setlist, the piece of
  gear, the take log, the invoice on its booking). The box keeps your words: tap it to get the results back, on the same
  tab. Nothing you type is kept once Tourbook is closed.

## Your first show

1. **Bookings → + New → Show.** Pick the act and the venue, the date, and the times: load-in, soundcheck, doors, on
   stage and the curfew. Type in the act and venue boxes: yours show in a list under the box to pick (capitals and
   accents don't matter, and a band's earlier names count). A name that isn't saved is kept as typed, and Tourbook asks
   whether to add it to your lists.
2. **Deal & fee:** a guarantee, a door split, or a guarantee versus a percentage. Tourbook shows what it pays at a quarter,
   half, three quarters and a full house.
3. **Advance checklist:** tick it off as you advance the show, or tap **Email the promoter** for an advance email that
   asks about everything still blank.
4. **Line-up & gear, setlist, guest list:** who's playing, what's going in the van, the set that fits your slot, and who's
   on the door list.
5. **Day sheet:** print it, or send the band a link with the times, address, line-up and setlist. It never shows money.
6. **On the night: Settlement.** Tickets, comps, merch counts and costs; Tourbook works out what the band made and each
   person's share.
7. **Quote & invoice:** made from the booking, in your currency or the client's, with Pay buttons in the link you send.

## Your first studio session

1. **Bookings → + New → Studio session.** The act or client, the studio, the times and your rate (per hour, per day,
   per song or a flat fee).
2. **Session clock:** tap **Start** when you begin, **Break** for lunch, **Stop** at the end. While it runs, a strip under
   the section menu shows it on every page.
3. **Take log:** tap **+ Take** for each take, mark the keepers, write what to fix.
4. **Songs and deliverables:** what you worked on, and the rough mix, final mix, master and stems with due dates.
5. **Quote & invoice:** the hours from the clock go straight onto the invoice.

## Keep your things safe

- **Everything stays on this device.** Nothing else leaves it but what you turn on or use: a venue's map and address
  search (OpenStreetMap); Google, from the moment you tap Connect Google Drive… (once connected, Drive syncs by itself);
  and, once licences are switched on, a working role's licence checks. The [privacy page](privacy.html) says in plain
  words what leaves your device and when.
- **Google Drive** (☁ → Backups → Connect Google Drive…) keeps every phone and computer you connect the same, with a
  dated copy each day for the last week and the last 12 months, in a hidden Tourbook folder of your own Drive that only Tourbook
  can open. It is free, whatever your role. Nothing goes to a Tourbook server.
- **The licence**, once it is switched on, is all that Tourbook's own server (tourbook.crowmade.app) ever hears about, and
  only once you use a working role, or a backup file or Google Drive brings in free days started on another device: an
  Audiophile who does neither sends it nothing.
- **Save a backup now and then:** tap ☁ at the top → **Save a backup**. Keep the file somewhere safe. To move to a new
  phone or computer, open Tourbook there and import the file. On a computer with Chrome or Edge you can also have Tourbook
  keep a copy in a file inside your Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive or iCloud Drive folder.
- **Links you send** (a day sheet, an invoice) carry the document inside the link itself, so no server sees them. Send
  them only to the people they are for.

## For the person who looks after the tech

- Tourbook is a plain web app with no build step: serve this repository's files from any static web host.
  `npm run serve` (or `node -e "import('./tests/lib.mjs').then(m => m.serve('.', 8124))"`) runs it on
  http://localhost:8124/.
- Before publishing a new version, run `node tools/stamp-build.mjs` so installed copies notice the update.
- The browser tests: `node tests/run.mjs` (needs Playwright and Chromium).
- [How it is built](docs/developers.md): the data, the sections, the booking boxes and who owns which file.
- Tourbook does not share anything with Vdex: its own storage (`tourbook-…` keys), its own offline cache, its own files, its
  own licence server, keys, Stripe link, Google project, desktop app id, port and download bucket.
- The licence server (`cloudflare/`) and the desktop app (`desktop/`) are built and tested, switched off. Google Drive
  is switched on in the repository (Tourbook's own Google sign-in client, since 15 November 2026: `docs/drive.md`):
  tourbook.crowmade.app gets it with its next publish, the desktop app with its first release.
  [The owner's go-live list](docs/go-live.md) says what to set up and in which order; nothing more is published without
  the owner's go-ahead. `TOURBOOK_LICENCE=on node tests/run.mjs` runs the tests with the licence live;
  `npm ci --prefix cloudflare` first lets the licence server's tests run.
